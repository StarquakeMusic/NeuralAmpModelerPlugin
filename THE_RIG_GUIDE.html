The Rig user guide

The Rig is a redesigned interface for Neural Amp Modeler. The plugin reads the information stored in each capture (what the gear is, who made it, how it was voiced) and draws that gear, with the whole interface coloured to match. The sound engine is unchanged. The cab gets one new tool: a mic you can move.

Step 0Installing on the Mojave machine

The zip has three builds plus this guide. They are Intel (x86_64) builds targeting macOS 10.14 and later. They aren't code-signed, so macOS will need to be told they're OK.

  1. Unzip NeuralAmpModeler-Mojave-x86_64.zip.
  2. Copy each file to its folder (create the folder if it doesn't exist):
    FileGoes inUsed by
    NeuralAmpModeler.vst3~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/Reaper, Ableton, Studio One, etc.
    NeuralAmpModeler.component~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/Logic, GarageBand, MainStage (Audio Unit)
    NeuralAmpModeler.app/Applications/ (or anywhere)Standalone, no DAW needed
  3. Clear the "downloaded from the internet" flag in Terminal so Gatekeeper doesn't block them: 
    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/NeuralAmpModeler.vst3
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/NeuralAmpModeler.component
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/NeuralAmpModeler.app
    For the app alone, right-click → Open → Open also works.
  4. For the Audio Unit: if Logic doesn't list it, quit Logic, run killall -9 AudioComponentRegistrar, and reopen Logic so it rescans.
  5. The standalone app asks for microphone access the first time. That's how it gets your guitar input, so allow it, then choose your interface in the app's audio settings.
Heads-up: these builds use the same name and plugin ID as the official Neural Amp Modeler, so installing them replaces it. Move the official copy somewhere safe first if you want to switch back. Saved sessions work in both directions; see What behaves differently.

OverviewA tour of the screen

The signal goes left to right, the same order the audio goes through the plugin: input meter → capture → cab and mic → output meter. The knobs live on a pedalboard underneath, with cables in the same order.

  • 1Scope: live waveform of what's coming out.
  • 2Fingerprint: how the capture compresses and clips.
  • 3Capture bay: the loaded gear, drawn from its metadata.
  • 4Cab / mic: the IR stage; drag the mic.
  • 5Model loader: pick or step through .nam files.
  • 6Library: browse a folder of captures as cards.
  • 7IR loader: pick or step through .wav IRs.
  • 8Pedalboard: input, gate, EQ, output and mix.
  • 9Meters: input (left) and output (right).
  • 10Settings: calibration, output mode, about.

Everyday useThe basics

  1. Load a capture. Click the folder icon on the model loader (5) and pick a .nam file. The < > arrows step through the other files in that folder. Click the file name for a menu of them all. × unloads the capture. The globe links to sites where you can get captures.
  2. Load a cab IR the same way on the IR loader (7). The waveform icon in the cab's top-right corner turns the whole cab stage on and off. When it's off, the cab shows BYPASSED.
  3. Set your level. Play and watch the left meter. Turn INPUT on the pedalboard until your loudest playing stays out of the red. The fingerprint's dot shows how hard you're hitting the capture.
  4. Shape it with the gate and the tone stack, then set OUTPUT and MIX (blend with your dry signal).
  5. Optional: move the mic on the cab, or open the library to try other captures quickly.

FeatureThe capture bay draws your gear

NAM captures usually store details about the gear they came from: type, make, model, tone, who captured it, and an estimate of how much gain it has. The bay uses them to draw the right kind of hardware, and the tone sets the colour of the whole interface (knob arcs, LEDs, meters, cables). The colour fades over when you switch.

Captures without this information still work. They show as an amp, use the file name as the title, and use the default colour.

NewThe fingerprint

When a capture loads, the plugin runs a test tone through it at 25 rising volumes and records what comes out. The result is a picture of how the gear behaves:

  • The curve: input level (left to right) against output level (bottom to top). The dotted line is a perfectly clean signal. The more the curve bends under it, the more the gear squashes and saturates. Clean amps track the line; high-gain amps flatten out early.
  • The wave: one cycle of the output at full drive. Round means clean; flat tops mean clipping.
  • COMP x:1: how much the top 24 dB of input is squeezed. 1:1 is untouched; 10:1 and above is heavily saturated.
  • BREAKS −xx dB: the input level where it starts to distort. "STAYS CLEAN" means it never does within the test range.
  • The glowing dot sits on the curve at the level you're playing right now.
Use it to: set INPUT by ear and eye. Push the dot into the bend for more breakup; keep it on the straight part for cleaner tones. Compare captures at a glance.

NewThe scope

The window at the top is a small oscilloscope of the plugin's output, in the tone colour. It locks onto the waveform so single notes hold still. Quiet signals are drawn smaller instead of being blown up to full height, so it also gives you a rough sense of level.

NewMic placement

The cab panel is the IR stage. The mic over the speaker can be moved, and moving it changes the sound.

  • Drag the mic outward from the centre (the dust cap) toward the edge of the cone. The header reads ON AXIS or OFF-AXIS 40%.
  • The slider on the right sets distance: NEAR at the top, FAR at the bottom. You can also scroll over the cab.
  • Double-click the speaker to recentre the mic, or double-click the slider to bring it back to near.

What it does to the sound

  • One IR loaded (or a capture that already includes its cab): moving off-axis rolls off the top end and adds a little low-mid body, the way a real mic does as it moves off the dust cap.
  • Two IRs: click + EDGE IR in the cab and pick a second .wav, ideally the same cab miked at the edge of the cone. A dashed ring marks the edge. Dragging from CAP to the ring then crossfades from your main IR to the edge IR (the header shows the mix, e.g. 20 / 80). Click × on the edge pill to remove it.
  • Distance: backing off loses proximity bass and some air.
Default: centre and near, which sounds exactly like before. Mic position and distance are automatable parameters (MicPosition, MicDistance), and both are saved with your session along with the edge IR.

NewThe pedalboard

The knobs are arranged as a pedalboard, in the order the audio flows: guitar → IN box → NOISE GATE → off to the capture (the cables going up) and back → TONE STACK → OUT box. Pulses travel along the cables while you play.

  • Footswitches turn the gate and the EQ on and off. The LED shows the state, and the pedal's knobs grey out when it's off.
  • THRESHOLD: the gate closes below this level. Raise it until hum between notes disappears.
  • BASS / MIDDLE / TREBLE: the tone stack after the capture, the same EQ as before.
  • INPUT, OUTPUT: levels in and out, in dB. MIX: blend of processed and dry signal (100% = fully processed).

NewThe capture library

Click LIBRARY next to the model loader. Every .nam in the current capture's folder (and two levels of subfolders) shows up as a card with a drawing of its gear, its tone, and who captured it. If nothing is loaded yet, it asks you to pick a folder.

  • Filter by gear (Amp, Amp + Cab, Pedal, Preamp, Studio) and by tone. Each chip shows how many captures match.
  • Click a card to load it; the library closes. The capture that's loaded is outlined and marked LOADED.
  • Pages: 9 cards per page. Use the < > buttons or the scroll wheel.
  • CHOOSE FOLDER switches to another folder (or rescans the same one). Esc or × closes the library.
Reading happens in the background, so big folders fill in as they're read ("SCANNING… 40 FOUND"). Up to 600 files are read.

Good to knowWhat behaves differently

WhatDetails
Window size960 × 540, up from the original 600 × 400. Drag the bottom-right corner to scale the whole interface.
FontsAmp logos use Brush Script and pedal labels use Impact, both from macOS. If either is missing, the bundled Michroma font is used instead.
Loading a captureTakes a moment longer than before, because the fingerprint is measured on load.
Cab stageWhen the IR button is on, the mic filters run even without an IR loaded, so captures that include their cab can use the mic too. At the default position they change nothing.
Saved sessionsThe edge IR and mic settings are saved after the normal data, behind a marker. Older sessions load with the mic centred and near, and the official NAM simply ignores the extra data.
Gate / EQ switchesSame on/off parameters as before, now footswitches on the pedals.
Settings pageUnchanged apart from the new background. It spreads out to fit the bigger window.

Honest statusNot yet tested

  • The builds compile and the screens were checked on a newer Mac running the Intel version, but they haven't run on a real Mojave machine yet. That's what this test is for.
  • The mic settings (how dark off-axis gets, how much distance removes) haven't been tuned by ear with a real guitar. Feedback welcome; they're easy to adjust.
  • Saving and reopening a DAW session with the edge IR and mic settings hasn't been tried in a host yet.

Things worth reporting back: whether each format loads in your DAW, whether the amp and pedal fonts show up, and how the mic feels.
